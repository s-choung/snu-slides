Computational Chemistry for Catalysis

Multiscale catalyst design integrating DFT, machine-learning potentials, and descriptors
Seokhyun ChoungJune 4, 2026
01
Motivation
1.1
Why Study Catalysis Computationally
Reaching what experiments cannot
  • Reaction intermediates live for only ps–ns, so in-situ observation is hard — computation delivers atomic-scale snapshots
  • Active-site candidates explode to 10⁴–10⁶ combinations — purely experimental screening is too costly and slow
  • Trace activity and selectivity back to their electronic-structure origin, distilling them into design principles

→ Computation accelerates hypothesis generation and candidate selection, letting experiments focus on validation — a closed loop.

Key idea: computation does not replace experiment — it narrows the search space
02
Methodology
2.1
A Multiscale Computational Stack
DFT
Adsorption energies, transition states, and the d-band center from first principles. The accuracy benchmark (ground truth), but it stalls at the few-hundred-atom scale.
ML Potentials
MLIPs (NequIP, MACE) trained on DFT give DFT accuracy at MD speed — extending to nanoparticles, dynamics, and ensemble sampling.
Microkinetics
Feed computed energies into rate equations to compare turnover frequency, the rate-determining step, and reaction orders directly with experiment.
A pipeline that bridges scales — from atoms to nanoparticles to reactor conditions
03
Design Principle
3.1
Descriptors and Scaling Relations
Compressing complex activity into a few variables
Scaling relations
Adsorbate energies are linearly coupled, so a single descriptor (e.g. O binding energy) can predict activity.
Sabatier volcano
Bind too weakly and nothing activates; bind too strongly and the surface poisons — an optimum exists.

04
Workflow
4.1
From ML Screening to Experiment
① Generate
Combine compositions, facets, and defects into tens of thousands of candidate structures; pre-filter stability with an MLIP.
② Predict
Regress activity and selectivity with descriptors and graph neural networks; quantify uncertainty to actively pick the next calculation (active learning).
③ Validate
Synthesize and test only the top candidates, return the data, and retrain — a closed loop that accelerates discovery.
Case: alloy oxygen-reduction catalysts compressed from 10,000 candidates to ~10 experiments
